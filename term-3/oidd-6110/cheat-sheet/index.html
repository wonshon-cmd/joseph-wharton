① 1 RESOURCE: flow rate · utilization · cycle time Capacity = m / p (×60 for /hr) · Flow rate R = min(Demand, Capacity)
Utilization = R / Capacity (≤100%) · Cycle time CT = 1 / R
Demand < cap → demand-constrained; else capacity-constrained (bottleneck 100%).
Laser cutter cap 5 jobs/hr, demand 4/hr → R = min(4,5) = 4/hr; u = 4/5 = 80%; CT = 1/4 hr = 15 min/job (not 60/5 = 12)

⑤ LITTLE'S LAW · INVENTORY TURNS · COST I = R × T (same time units) · T = I/R
ER 180 patients/day, 60 inside → T = 60/180 day = 8 h
Turns = COGS / Inventory (not revenue) · Days of supply = Inv/COGS × 365 = 365/turns
Per-unit inventory cost = (annual %) / turns × item cost
Rev 3,450, COGS 2,070, Inv 345 → turns 2,070/345 = 6; 24%/6 = 4% → $150 item: $6.00; days = 365/6 = 60.8
True: higher turns ⇒ shorter time in inventory ⇒ lower per-unit holding cost. Not implied: higher revenue, margin or profit (Tiffany 0.66 turns, 61% margin; Kroger 13.4). Compare competitors only.

⑥ BUILD-UP DIAGRAM (inventory / backlog over time) Slope = supply rate − demand rate (+ = inventory, − = backlog)
Convert: demand/hr = customers × units each / hours open
Last customer served = start + total units / production rate (if backlog at end)
Level at time t = P·(t − start) − D·(t − open); max backlog = level when arrivals stop
Runs out when P(t − s) = D(t − open) → t = (D·open − P·s)/(D − P)

⑧ WAITING (variability) · TRANSIENT QUEUE a = avg time BETWEEN arrivals (30/hr → a = 2 min) · u = p/(a·m) (<1) · CV = sd/mean (Poisson CVa = 1)
Tq = (p/m) · u√(2(m+1))−1/(1−u) · (CVa²+CVp²)/2; m=1: p·u/(1−u)·(…)/2
T = Tq + p · Iq = Tq/a · Ip = p/a · I = Iq + Ip
