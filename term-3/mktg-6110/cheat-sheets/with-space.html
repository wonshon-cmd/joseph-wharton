④ EXTENSIONS & LUXURY ★ sample essay
  • Why extend: ① cheaper (leverage equity) ② move the brand (Mountain Man → young drinkers)
  • Two-way test: brand adds value to the extension AND the extension adds value back. Never extend a weak brand or weaken the original → otherwise use a new brand.
  • Horizontal (new category, same price: Clorox wipes, Lulu men) vs vertical (BMW; going down hurts the top; Toyota→Lexus; Kia)
  • Luxury: does it reinforce or dilute prestige/scarcity? Too accessible → loses cachet → loses the premium. Pierre Cardin ✘ vs Hermès/Gucci ✔. Long-term equity > short-term revenue.
  • Stay fresh: Schweppes heritage · Pepsi music · Porsche gaming · LV × Supreme · silver bullet
  • Line ext. = existing brand + existing category · Category ext. = existing brand + new category · Multi-brand = new brand + existing category · new + new = new brand
  • Ext. risks: stretched too many ways → loses meaning · old name repels the young (they rebel vs parents' brands) · alienates or cannibalizes the core (check BECR)
  • Reposition when target market changes, competitors move, context shifts, brand ages (“never let a brand age out”), NOT to cut COGS. Rewrite target/POD/FOR/RTB → 4Ps
  • Silver bullet = radically different move to grab attention and restart (riskiest, last resort)

▸ e.g. Clorox → wipes · Starbucks → bottled Frappuccino, ice cream · Lululemon women → men · BMW 760Li $160k vs 128i $31.5k · Mountain Man light beer · Pierre Cardin ✘ · Hermès, Gucci ✔ · Schweppes · Pepsi · Porsche · LV × Supreme · Amazon luxury (doubtful)

⑤ SUBSCRIPTION PERKS ★ sample essay

⑥ MASS vs TWO SEGMENTS ★ sample essay (25% each)
  • Mass: cheaper, efficient media, less tailored · Segments: more relevant, costlier, complex
  • Depends on: cost of differentiation (check ROI) · competitive intensity (high → segment) · brand equity (strong like Coke → mass OK)
  • Awareness-first (Coke, Bud; double jeopardy) vs passion-first niche (Lulu; cheaper with fragmented media)
  • Limited budget: concentrate on the best-fit segment first, prove ROI, then add
  • Double jeopardy: big brands get more buyers AND those buyers buy more often → broad awareness still pays in mass/average categories

▸ e.g. Coca-Cola, Budweiser (mass awareness) · Lululemon (niche → mass) · TV Guide (known, undifferentiated) · Quidel (2 segments) · Pringles (vague → kids/teens)

⑦ MEASURING BRANDS 🎤
  • Customer-based: awareness (recognition, recall, top-of-mind, latency) → associations (conjoint) → emotion (neuromarketing) → loyalty (purchases). Price premium = branded − unbranded price.
  • BAV: Differentiation (margins), Relevance (trial), Esteem (loyalty), Knowledge. Strength = D+R; Stature = E+K.
  • Opposite models: awareness first (Coke, Bud) vs passion first (BAV, Lululemon). Known but undifferentiated = weak (TV Guide); niche may not scale.
  • Interbrand: brand earnings, discounted; strong brand → low rate → high value. Used to justify spend to the CFO, in M&A, in lawsuits. Coke $68B → Apple #1.
  • Brand (Scott Cook): formally a trademark · conceptually a promise · realistically what consumers tell each other. Vodka $11–$44 → any commodity can be differentiated
  • CBBE order: Awareness → Associations → Attitudes → Attachment (who are you? what are you? what about you? you & me?)
  • Conjoint: people are bad at stating importance → choices among bundles (brand × price × features) reveal brand utility
  • BAV power grid: hi strength + hi stature = Leadership · hi strength, lo stature = unrealized potential (niche) · lo strength, hi stature = eroding · lo/lo = new or dead
  • Interbrand = intangible earnings × role of brand, discounted at a brand-strength rate; DCF to yr 10 + residual. Profit firms only (non-profits need a substitute metric)
  • Brand building = continuous, cultural, meaning-driven vs AIDA selling = episodic, individual, attribute-driven. Most brands over-invest in the bottom funnel (measurable)

▸ e.g. Coke $68B → Apple $470.9B #1 · Nvidia +116% · Tesla −35% · perfume 95% vs utilities 0% · Lululemon (BAV) · TV Guide

⑧ PRICING BASICS 🎤

EVC
  • Pool pill: EVC $20 + $200 = $220+, but real ≈ $10–20 (fairness: $0.50 chlorine; rivals undercut)
  • Cost: markup (Costco 11%, Walmart 25%) or break-even (incl. fixed)
  • Fixed-heavy → LOWER price (“cover the fixed”: 1,000 @ $7 = $1,500; 750 = $0) · variable-heavy → RAISE (omakase)
  • Competition: can AND will they match? Warby $95 vs Luxottica $300: no
  • EVC = rival's lifecycle cost − our start-up − our post-purchase + our incremental value = max WTP. Exclude non-incremental items (pool cover)
  • EVC common in B2B: savings are quantifiable → raises the customer's reference point. No rivals + R&D story (pharma) → price near EVC
  • Company factors: maximize profit (discrimination), cover costs, product-line consistency (premium ≥ cheap version) vs competitive factors (can/will they respond)

▸ e.g. Raju's pool pill · Warby $95 vs Luxottica $300 · Costco 11% / Walmart 25% · Jesse's omakase · BeWood

MARKET VALUE · WACC · BETA
  • Market value = future free cash flow discounted at WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital = blended return debt + equity investors require)
  • WACC ↑ → value ↓: 1–2 pts ≈ 20–30% of value. Debt cheap at first, too much → risk ↑ → WACC ↑
  • ROIC > WACC = value created; growth with ROIC < WACC destroys value (prove the spread, then scale)
  • Beta (β) = how much the stock moves with the market → β ↑ → cost of equity ↑ → WACC ↑
  • Strong brand + loyal customers → steadier cash flow → lower risk, β, WACC → higher value. CLV discounts the same way (i ≈ WACC)

⑨ PRICE DISCRIMINATION 🎤
  • 1st: a price per person (haggling: cars, art, B2B; control the sales force)
  • 2nd: self-select/quantity: bulk · 2-part (razor+blades, HP ink, Keurig) · 3-part (subscriptions)
  • 3rd: by segment: skim (Apple, fashion) vs penetration (freemium, AI) · EDLP + price-match vs high-low (loss leaders, cherry-pickers) · random (coupons) vs periodic (early bird) · student ID · good/better/best
  • Good/better/best: “best” priced above its extra benefits' value (insensitive buyers, like 1st class); most pick the middle. Student ID = fence (transaction cost)
  • Price-match signals “cheapest” → most shoppers stop searching; the few searchers get matched vs rivals' loss leaders. Periodic (early bird) = give up time

▸ e.g. Used cars, art (1st) · razors, HP ink, Keurig, newspapers (2nd) · Apple, runway → T.J.Maxx, AI freemium, Walmart price-match, early bird, student ID, first class (3rd)

⑩ PLACE & CHANNELS 🎤
  • 30–50% of price in the channel; now the “favorite P” (differentiation). Breadth = # categories, depth = # choices.
  • Direct: margin, control, DATA · hard to scale. Indirect: scale · double marginalization, channel conflict (car dealers; Tesla “not a car”)
  • Nike DTC 2020 → HOKA/On/NB won → 2024 rebuild wholesale. Retail failures = stores + inventory + debt.
  • Double marginalization = each middleman marks up separately → higher end price AND less total profit; channel often earns more than the brand
  • DTC risks: hard to scale, logistics on your balance sheet, costly acquisition, needs awareness, buyers want to compare. Nike shoppers switched brands in-store, didn't go online
  • It depends: DTC brands (Bonobos, Rent the Runway) → Nordstrom/Amazon for scale; luxury leaves dept stores for control; many do both (hybrid)
  • “Favorite P”: mature categories differentiate via experience (Sephora vs Macy's). Data ownership = key negotiation point. Kirkland = private (store) brand

▸ e.g. Nike DTC → lost to HOKA, On, New Balance · Tesla vs dealers · Amazon data · Sephora vs Macy's · Bonobos → Nordstrom · Kirkland · Apple, Lululemon

CLV (slides)
CLV = m × r ÷ (1 + i − r) − AC
  • Phone: AC $400; m $250; 1-yr BE 1.6M custs · 5 yrs $1,250 → churn $840 → discount $664 (net $264)
  • Avon: avg $0.87B vs segments $1.89B → averages mislead · CAC ≤ CLV · 80/20
  • Funnel: everything before the 1st purchase = acquisition cost; after = CLV. CLV = NPV of future profit − AC (customers as assets)
  • M-O-F: Marketing CAC ≤ CLV · Ops sets service levels by segment · Finance treats customers as a measurable asset (Marketing–Finance interface)
  • Formula assumes constant m & r, year-end cash, infinite horizon (NOT growing revenue). Phone = pure single-service contractual (Fader)
  • Lever 1 Costs & Fit: fire low-CLV customers, self-service, referrals · Lever 2: premium tiers, cross/upsell, lock-in. CLV nonlinear in r → averages hide loyal segments

▸ e.g. Mobile carrier · Avon-style seller · Costco · Diaper Club ($438) · Harrah's

⑨+ PRICE SENSITIVITY · DYNAMIC · SUBSCRIPTIONS 🎤
  • Want inelastic. Less sensitive: no substitutes/necessity · brand = quality signal · switching costs · hard to compare · 3rd party pays · mental accounting · scarcity (OK for luxury, PR risk at Walmart) · loyalty. More: stockpiling, easy comparison.
  • Bundle: sensitive on main item, not peripherals (= loss leader logic); end-of-aisle hides substitutes
  • Dynamic → 1st-degree; fairness: Uber ✔ (labeled from start, 2-sided: more drivers) vs Wendy's/Walmart ✘ · surge vs gouging
  • Subs: replenish · curated box (revenue = full-size buys) · tiered. Iyengar: self-selection; frequency ↑, basket ↓, fades; ~25% unprofitable → differentiated incentives
  • Dynamic pricing fix (QSR): frame as off-peak discounts, not peak surcharges; be transparent, test. Airlines/Amazon OK because customers expect it
  • Margin analysis: ALWAYS (profit pools, channel/rival incentives). Inputs best→worst: hard numbers → averages → educated guesses → wishful thinking (sensitivity only)
  • Total BE = fixed ÷ unit contribution: screens out losers when forecasts are weak (first step only). Ads + price cut need MORE than the sum (ads recovered at smaller margin)
  • BECR = new UC ÷ old UC = max share of new sales from own products (actual must be lower). Reciprocal 1.33 = 1 cannibalized + 0.33 incremental
  • New UC > old → BECR >100%, no problem. Distinct brand/pack/channel lowers actual cannibalization. BECR is an input, not the decision (strategy, sensitivity)
  • Acuvue One Day: $0.40 vs $2.00/lens but $292 vs $208/customer-yr → denominator = binding constraint (capacity → per lens; customers → per customer)
  • ECPs earn less ($131 vs $208), need more effort, unfair split → won't push (fix: cut price to ECPs). Retailers push whichever brand earns them more
  • US benchmarks: channel margin 25–50% of end price · unit margin 20–40% · marketing 10–20% of sales · ads ~5%
  • Chain model = pop × seg % × penetration × usage × UC × share · wCPM = cost ÷ target-weighted reach (000s)

▸ e.g. pharma · Wells Fargo · insurance, biz class · Paris vs Philly · Hermès Birkin, Ferrari · Amazon comps · Uber, Wendy's · Harry's, Birchbox, Prime

⑪ PROMOTION 🎤
  • IMC = all tools, one message (empowered consumers, fragmented media, silos waste $). WOM = 13% online / 26% offline of sales impact.
  • 6Ms: Market → Mission (SMART) → Message (Awareness → Persuasion → Conversion) → Media → Money (S-curve) → Measurement. Market first (Taco Bell breakfast).
  • Ad elasticity only ≈0.1 (promos big but no long-term growth) → advertise for equity + mental availability, search, pricing power (Coca-Cola)
  • Attribution = split credit (online only) · Incrementality = holdout test, would they buy anyway? · MMM = historical, privacy-proof, budget allocation
  • Paid (buy) · Owned (control) · Earned (win) · Retail media (sell; 1st-party data at purchase; $71B, Amazon/Walmart/Target ~74%)
  • 🎤 Class: performance vs brand (promos erode margin; brand equity drives purchase) · earned = gold standard, needs paid first · Taco Bell 3 targets (rivals' breakfast, own lunch fans, non-breakfast) → each its own campaign; “Routine Republic” + Waze: 15% visited, 1 in 7 returned · last-touch → multi-touch → MMM (multicollinearity, endogeneity) · GDPR/CCPA · S-curve threshold ~3 exposures · Coke keeps advertising (salience) · Budweiser spikes at later games · Duolingo: 5-sec SB slot + in-app push
  • Elasticities: ads ≈0.1 · price ≈ −2.6 · promos −2 to −12 (pull-forward) · distribution ≈1. Organic growth: distribution, innovation, satisfaction, brand
  • SMART = Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Knows brand but won't consider it → Persuasion (interest, evaluation)
  • S-curve: below threshold ≈ wasted · steep middle · diminishing returns past saturation
  • Incrementality = (test − control) ÷ test; randomized holdout or PSA placebo, mostly online. Last-click over-credits search; MMM slow, no individual data
  • Ad→WOM study: +10% TV ≈ +69K mentions, but 1% WOM → only 0.01–0.06% sales (more in entertainment/sports). YouTube pays 12–24 months
  • Watermarking (LG Ads): IDs at each touchpoint for privacy-compliant tracking; needs publisher cooperation

▸ e.g. Taco Bell · Dunkin' SMART · Coca-Cola · Super Bowl $8M (WOM spikes, decays) · TikTok 42% ROI, YouTube long tail · gen AI: 45% of young consumers like AI ads vs 82% execs assume
