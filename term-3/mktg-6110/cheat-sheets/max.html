① KAHN RETAILING SUCCESS MATRIX ★ sample MC
SAMPLE Amazon = best at Frictionless, leveraging it to be best at Low Price, fair value at Brand & Customer Experience. ✘ “Best at Experience & Brand” · ✘ “Best at Low Price & Brand” · ✘ “Best at Experience → Brand”.

Why it exists: the “retail apocalypse.” Customers go where they get the best value on what they care about; there are only 4 ways to offer superior value.

Arrow = start strength → leveraged 2nd quadrant. Brand: products pull you in (brands, design, curation). Experiential: fun (discovery, service, community). Low Price: operational excellence (EDLP, lean, cut middlemen). Frictionless: easiest (no lines, fast, one-stop, data personalization).

  1. Survive = at least fair value (customers' minimum) in ALL 4. Below in any one → leave (RadioShack failed).
  2. Lead = best in 1 → leverage into a 2nd (“arrow” strategy).
  3. Don't overspend in the other 2; “good enough” is enough (extra spend drains your strengths).
  4. Bar keeps rising: a leader's edge becomes everyone's new minimum (2-day delivery). Old view “best at 1” is no longer enough.
Trap: Amazon & Walmart = SAME 2 quadrants, OPPOSITE start. “Best at X, leveraging to Y”: X = starting strength.

CLASS Sim: Getchells = Macy's, mid-tier dept store “stuck in the middle” (Sephora, TJX, Walmart, Costco win). Fair value = 75% of leader. Spending moves you only if you outspend rivals over time (“sluggish”). Levers: online → F · shopping exp. → E · service → F+E · vendor mgmt → LP (best long-term; “manage costs even if not low price”) · brand quality/private brands → B · analytics = booster · stores = access, not preference. Best teams: lead 2 + parity 2.

e.g. Brand: Nordstrom, Best Buy, Kroger, LV, Hermès, Lululemon, Glossier · Exp: Sephora, Apple Store, Eataly, Whole Foods, Trader Joe's, Stitch Fix, Duane Reade · LP: Walmart, Costco, T.J.Maxx, Burlington · F: Amazon · Sim: Cadabra=Amazon, PriceMart=Walmart, AIM=Target, BestCo=Costco, Zephara=Sephora, KT Benny=JCPenney
② WHAT MAKES A GOOD SEGMENTATION ★ sample MC
SAMPLE East/west by geography: good? → “It depends on whether consumers WITHIN each division were relatively homogeneous in their reactions to marketing variables and the two divisions were sufficiently DIFFERENT from each other.”
Why the others are wrong: “distinct, no overlap” (separate on a map ≠ behave differently) · “geography never good” (too absolute: climate, culture) · “hard to observe/reach” (geography is EASY) · “heterogeneity WITHIN” = backwards.

Segmentation = split the market into groups to serve each well. “Serving the average buyer well serves no one”

Good segment: ✔ homogeneous within (react the same to the 4Ps) ✔ heterogeneous between ✔ identifiable ✔ reachable ✔ big/profitable enough ✔ actionable (“only valuable if actionable”). Firms uncover, not create, segments (reasons-why/AI, interviews, cluster, conjoint, latent class).

④ BRAND EXTENSIONS & LUXURY ★ sample essay
SAMPLE Gucci/Hermès careful vs Pierre Cardin overextended: how to weigh long-term equity vs short-term revenue? Grading: does it reinforce or dilute prestige/scarcity? Short-term profit, but going too far into mass market isn't worth it. Must mention luxury: too accessible / too available → loses cachet → loses luxury premium.

Extension = famous name on a new product. CLASS 2 advantages: ① leverage equity (“much cheaper to introduce”) ② move the brand (Mountain Man: older beer → young drinkers).

“Never extend a brand if it's weak… never extend if it's gonna weaken the original… two strong links.”

Horizontal = new category, similar price (Clorox → wipes; Starbucks → bottled Frappuccino; Lulu women → men: women bought men's clothes first; Kahn wrongly doubted it). Risk: stretch too many ways → “lost what it originally meant.” Vertical = same category, higher/lower price (BMW $160k vs $31.5k). Down sells volume but damages the top; up may not be credible → Toyota made Lexus; Kia tries own name; Amazon luxury? doubtful.

Luxury = scarcity/exclusivity · heritage (“can't be European luxury unless you're European luxury”: 200 yrs = lasting advantage) · price premium (perfume 90–95% brand). Pierre Cardin: licensed everywhere → quick money, lost cachet. Hermès/Gucci extend only where the brand stays rare.

Essay framework: ① fit: reinforce or dilute? ② two-way test ③ direction (down = danger) ④ short-term gain (+ cannibalization risk) ⑤ long-term cost (measure: price premium, BAV, Interbrand) ⑥ alternatives (new/endorsed/sub-brand).

Keep fresh CLASS: Schweppes old & tired in COVID cocktail boom → heritage made modern · Pepsi music (incubates young musicians) · Porsche gaming showrooms · LV × Supreme · silver bullet (radical, risky). “Young people always rebel against what their parents do.”

⑤ SUBSCRIPTION PERKS ★ sample essay
SAMPLE Free shipping, gifts, exclusive deals: 2 unintended consequences? Grading: ↑ signups but erode margins & train customers to expect discounts; some buy only when offers exist → reduces incremental value, may cannibalize full-price sales.
CLV = m × r ÷ (1 + i − r) − AC

m = yearly profit/customer · r = retention · i = discount (10%) · AC = acquisition cost. Multiplier r/(1.1−r): r .8 → 2.67×; Costco ~.92 → 5.1×.

Perks pay only if retention rises enough: here need ~86% to break even. Sign-ups alone prove nothing.

4 consequences: ① margin erosion (cost every order; heavy users cost most) ② trained to expect discounts (reference price ↓, wait for deals) ③ deal-seekers → low incrementality ④ cannibalize full-price sales (loyal buyers get discount for nothing).

CLASS Student: discount loyal buyers? Kahn: “Oh, you do that? Not me. I raise price.” Loyal = less price-sensitive (American Airlines in Philly: pay “crazy” prices for “three peanuts”). Subscriptions = 3-part tariff (2nd-degree); freemium = penetration.

Better: tiers/thresholds · non-price perks (access, service, experiences) · measure incrementality vs control group · protect full-price integrity (esp. premium/luxury).

⑧ PRICING BASICS: CEILING, FLOOR, IN BETWEEN

EVC = value in $, in the customer's terms; common in B2B (software, services, energy); consumer = more branding. Showing it raises the reference point even if you don't get it (EVs vs gas; efficient appliances).

Pool pill (Prof. Raju, 20,000 gal): drain $1/1,000 = $20 + refill $1/100 = $200 = $220 + headache (student: $450). Real price ≈ $10–20: ① fairness (~$0.50 chlorine) ② competitive rivalry (“$200 margin? I'll come in at $100” → $50…). No rivals + R&D story (pharma) → near EVC.

Floor, 2 ways: markup (cost + %: Costco ~11%, Walmart ~25%) vs break-even (each unit carries share of fixed: “really covering your cost”). Do both in BeWood.

CLV (slides)

CLV = profit from a customer over all years in today's $ (margin × retention, discounted, minus acquisition cost). Concepts: 1-yr break-even looks bad, lifetime view looks good; retention & discounting shrink value; averages mislead (Avon: segments $1.89B vs average $0.87B). Uses: Mktg CAC ≤ CLV · Ops: service by segment · Fin: customers = asset. 80/20. Levers: cut cost-to-serve, fire low-CLV / invest in high-CLV, cross/upsell, lock-in.

⑨+ PRICE SENSITIVITY · BUNDLES · DYNAMIC · SUBSCRIPTIONS (exam review)

Elastic = small price change → big demand change · Inelastic = price ↑, people still buy. Marketers WANT inelastic.

Scarcity: fine for luxury; legal at Walmart but ethical/PR risk. Fairness decides.

Bundle < sum of parts: sensitive on main item (computer), insensitive on peripherals → margin there = loss-leader logic (grocery). End-of-aisle display removes visible substitutes.

Dynamic pricing → toward 1st-degree, captures surplus; acceptance = fairness. Uber ✔ (labeled from start + 2-sided: ↑price → ↑drivers) vs Wendy's/Walmart ✘ backlash (exploit need). Airlines/Amazon routine. Surge vs gouging = perceived alternatives + fairness.

Subscriptions: replenishment (Harry's) · curated box (Birchbox: $ from full-size buys) · tiered loyalty.

Iyengar loyalty study (Asian beauty co.): endogeneity / self-selection (Prime members spend more because heavy spenders join). Program: frequency ↑, basket slightly ↓, effect fades; ~25% of members = no profit → heterogeneity: use data for differentiated incentives, mind fairness.
