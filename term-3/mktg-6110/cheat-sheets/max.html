① KAHN RETAILING SUCCESS MATRIX ★ sample MC
SAMPLE Amazon = best at Frictionless, leveraging it to be best at Low Price, fair value at Brand & Customer Experience. ✘ “Best at Experience & Brand” · ✘ “Best at Low Price & Brand” · ✘ “Best at Experience → Brand”.

Why it exists: the “retail apocalypse.” Customers go where they get the best value on what they care about; there are only 4 ways to offer superior value.

Arrow = start strength → leveraged 2nd quadrant. Brand: products pull you in (brands, design, curation). Experiential: fun (discovery, service, community). Low Price: operational excellence (EDLP, lean, cut middlemen). Frictionless: easiest (no lines, fast, one-stop, data personalization).

  1. Survive = at least fair value (customers' minimum) in ALL 4. Below in any one → leave (RadioShack failed).
  2. Lead = best in 1 → leverage into a 2nd (“arrow” strategy).
  3. Don't overspend in the other 2; “good enough” is enough (extra spend drains your strengths).
  4. Bar keeps rising: a leader's edge becomes everyone's new minimum (2-day delivery). Old view “best at 1” is no longer enough.
Trap: Amazon & Walmart = SAME 2 quadrants, OPPOSITE start. “Best at X, leveraging to Y”: X = starting strength.

④ BRAND EXTENSIONS & LUXURY ★ sample essay
SAMPLE Gucci/Hermès careful vs Pierre Cardin overextended: how to weigh long-term equity vs short-term revenue? Grading: does it reinforce or dilute prestige/scarcity? Short-term profit, but going too far into mass market isn't worth it. Must mention luxury: too accessible / too available → loses cachet → loses luxury premium.

Extension = famous name on a new product. CLASS 2 advantages: ① leverage equity (“much cheaper to introduce”) ② move the brand (Mountain Man: older beer → young drinkers).

“Never extend a brand if it's weak… never extend if it's gonna weaken the original… two strong links.”

Horizontal = new category, similar price (Clorox → wipes; Starbucks → bottled Frappuccino; Lulu women → men: women bought men's clothes first; Kahn wrongly doubted it). Risk: stretch too many ways → “lost what it originally meant.” Vertical = same category, higher/lower price (BMW $160k vs $31.5k). Down sells volume but damages the top; up may not be credible → Toyota made Lexus; Kia tries own name; Amazon luxury? doubtful.

Luxury = scarcity/exclusivity · heritage (“can't be European luxury unless you're European luxury”: 200 yrs = lasting advantage) · price premium (perfume 90–95% brand). Pierre Cardin: licensed everywhere → quick money, lost cachet. Hermès/Gucci extend only where the brand stays rare.

Brand × category grid: existing brand + existing category = line extension · existing brand + new category = category extension · new brand + existing category = multi-brands · new + new = new brand. Reposition when target market, competitors, or context change, or to stay fresh (“never let the brand age out”): Apple Computer → Apple Inc. (2007, iPhone day).

Essay framework: ① fit: reinforce or dilute? ② two-way test ③ direction (down = danger) ④ short-term gain (+ cannibalization risk) ⑤ long-term cost (measure: price premium, BAV, Interbrand) ⑥ alternatives (new/endorsed/sub-brand).

CLV (slides)

CLV = profit from a customer over all years in today's $ (margin × retention, discounted, minus acquisition cost). Concepts: 1-yr break-even looks bad, lifetime view looks good; retention & discounting shrink value; averages mislead (Avon: segments $1.89B vs average $0.87B). Uses: Mktg CAC ≤ CLV · Ops: service by segment · Fin: customers = asset. 80/20. Funnel: everything before 1st purchase = acquisition cost; after = CLV. Simple formula assumes constant m and r, year-end cash, infinite horizon (no growth). Lever 1 Costs & Fit: cut cost-to-serve, fire low-CLV, self-service, referrals/WOM. Lever 2 Revenue & Loyalty: premium tiers, cross/upsell, lock-in ecosystems. Fader models: mobile = pure contractual; also non-contractual, hybrid, 2-sided.

⑧ PRICING BASICS: CEILING, FLOOR, IN BETWEEN

EVC = value in $, in the customer's terms; common in B2B (software, services, energy); consumer = more branding. Showing it raises the reference point even if you don't get it (EVs vs gas; efficient appliances).

Pool pill (Prof. Raju, 20,000 gal): drain $1/1,000 = $20 + refill $1/100 = $200 = $220 + headache (student: $450). Real price ≈ $10–20: ① fairness (~$0.50 chlorine) ② competitive rivalry (“$200 margin? I'll come in at $100” → $50…). No rivals + R&D story (pharma) → near EVC.

Floor, 2 ways: markup (cost + %: Costco ~11%, Walmart ~25%) vs break-even (each unit carries share of fixed: “really covering your cost”).

Widget ($7, VC $1, fixed $4,500): 1,000 units → $1,500 profit; 750 → $0.

Right answer: LOWER price: fixed-heavy → need volume, “cover the fixed” (raising loses $ at −5% or −33% volume). Variable-heavy → RAISE (Jesse's omakase: fish + chef time per seat; cheap front room covers the building).

Company factors: ① max profit (price discrimination) ② cover costs ③ product-line logic (premium can't be below cheap). Competition = game theory: CAN they match (costs)? WILL they? Warby $95 vs Luxottica ~$300: could crush, wouldn't (too much margin at stake, can't raise back; CEO waved it off). Raju advised Warby's founder.

RTB = Reason to Believe: proof of the POD

Frame of ref. = Frame of Reference: what you're compared against

B / E / LP / F = Kahn Matrix: Brand / Experiential / Low Price / Frictionless

B2B · B2C = Business-to-Business (sell to firms) · Business-to-Consumer

CPG = Consumer Packaged Goods: soap, snacks, drinks

Money & math:

CLV = Customer Lifetime Value: profit from one customer over all years, in today's $

CAC · AC = (Customer) Acquisition Cost: $ to win one customer

m · r · i = yearly margin · retention rate (% who stay) · discount rate

UC = Unit Contribution: price − variable cost (profit per unit)

VC · OH = Variable Cost (per unit) · Overhead (fixed running costs)

BE = Break-Even: sales where profit = $0

BECR = Break-Even Cannibalization Rate: max % of a cheaper new product's sales that may come from your own old product

EVC = Economic Value to the Customer: max a fully informed customer should pay (price ceiling)

WTP = Willingness To Pay

wCPM = weighted Cost Per Thousand: ad cost per 1,000 TARGET people reached

Marketing terms:

IMC = Integrated Marketing Communications: all channels, one message

6Ms = Market, Mission, Message, Media, Money, Measurement

SMART = Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound

AIDA = Awareness, Interest, Desire, Action

MMM = Marketing Mix Model: stats on years of spend + sales data

PSA = Public Service Announcement (placebo ad in tests)

POP = Point of Purchase (in-store display, packaging)

eWOM = electronic (online) word of mouth

QSR = Quick-Service Restaurant (fast food)

SKU = Stock-Keeping Unit: one specific product version

SoM = Share of Model: how often/favorably AI chatbots name your brand

EDLP = Everyday Low Price: steady low prices (Walmart)

DTC = Direct-to-Consumer: brand sells to you itself, no store in between

WOM = Word of Mouth

BAV = BrandAsset Valuator (Young & Rubicam): brand-health scorecard

SEO · GEO = Search / Generative Engine Optimization: show up on Google vs in AI-chat answers

Company short forms:

P&G = Procter & Gamble

LVMH = Louis Vuitton Moët Hennessy
